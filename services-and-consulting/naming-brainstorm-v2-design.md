# Naming Brainstorm v2 — The Design Vein

Second pass. v1 (`naming-brainstorm-v1-craft-trades.md`) is kept for its diagnosis of `ndustrio.us` and its constraint set, both of which carry forward unchanged.

---

## What went wrong in v1

The instinct was right — *craft* — but it was sourced from the wrong workshop. Keel, Shipwright, Millwright, Whetstone, Plumbline all come from **the shipyard and the job site**: a trades register. Honest and masculine and durable, but it reads *contractor*, not *studio*. A founder hearing those names pictures someone who builds what you spec. A design-heavy firm needs the founder to picture someone who decides *what should exist*.

Design studios name themselves from a different vocabulary, and it's remarkably consistent:

- **Print, type, and bookmaking** — Colophon, Imprint, Folio, Signature, Character
- **Plain deadpan nouns** — Instrument, Manual, Method, Standard, Object, Field, Order
- **Material and surface** — Vellum, Slate, Gesso, Patina, Linen
- **Light, optics, form** — Prism, Aperture, Umbra

Notice what's absent: tools, trades, verbs of exertion. Design names are **nouns of the made thing and the medium it's made in**, not the labor that made it.

### The lucky part

For a Christian design firm this is a gift, because the richest well in that vocabulary is one where Christian devotion and design craft were *the same discipline for a thousand years*: **the illuminated manuscript and the cathedral.** Monks in a scriptorium making books that were good, beautiful, and true — literally your three words, literally as a design practice. Every name below in veins A and B comes from there. It's design-world credible on its face and devotionally load-bearing underneath, without ever announcing itself.

### What survives from v1

**Cairn** still works — it's abstract, material, and reads studio rather than site. **Cubit** half-survives; it's a measure, which is design-adjacent, but it's arithmetic where the design world wants substance. Everything else in v1 I'd retire.

## Phase 2 — Candidates

### A. Print, type & bookmaking

| Name | Rationale | Domain prior | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Colophon** | The printer's mark at the back of a book naming who made it, on what press, in what type. It is the craftsman's signature on finished work — **and a colophon only ever appears on a book that got finished.** "A prototype isn't a product," compressed into one word. Designers revere colophons. | `.com` likely held; `colophon.studio` | Colophon Foundry (London type house) is well known in exactly your audience — a design-world collision, not a legal one, but real. 3 syllables and some radio spelling risk |
| **Vellum** | The prepared skin that everything meant to last was written on. Material, tactile, immediately design-world; and in the same breath it's the surface of every surviving manuscript. Two syllables, spells itself from hearing. | `vellum.com` likely held; `vellum.studio`, `vellumco.com` | Vellum is established book-formatting software (vellum.pub) — check Class 42 |
| **Rubric** | The red initial that opened a manuscript page; *also* the standard a work is judged against; *also* the instruction in red telling you how the liturgy is rightly done. Three meanings, all yours. | Good `.studio`/`.co` odds | **Rubrik** (NYSE: RBRK, enterprise data security) is a homophone with a multi-billion-dollar brand. Every spoken mention leaks to them. Heartbreaking, but this is probably disqualifying for an audio-first brand |
| **Folio** | A sheet of a manuscript; the format of the First Folio. Elegant, short, perfect radio test, unmistakably from the world of made books. | `.com` held; `folio.studio` | Common in publishing — harder to own, more search competition |
| **Imprint** | A publisher's mark, and the act of leaving something lasting. Zero spelling risk, plain English, design-legible. | `.com` held; `imprint.studio` | The most generic of this vein; several existing uses |
| **Signature** | In bookbinding, the folded gathering that becomes a book — the unit a book is actually assembled from. Plus the obvious: your mark on the work. | Poor — very common word | Hard to own or search. Include for completeness |
| **Ligature** | Where two letterforms are cut into a single form. A two-person studio where engineering and design aren't a handoff. | Good odds | Surgical connotation drags; slightly cold |

### B. Cathedral & sacred architecture

| Name | Rationale | Domain prior | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tracery** | The carved stone ribwork of a cathedral window — the structure that holds the glass. **It is simultaneously the load-bearing element and the most beautiful thing in the building**, which is the entire argument for a firm where engineering and design are one team. Also *trace*: drawing an idea into form. | Decent `.com` odds; `tracery.studio` | 3 syllables and slightly delicate; a small open-source JS library shares the name (Kate Compton's generative grammar tool) — no company conflict |
| **Plinth** | The base a made object stands on. Short, dry, design-gallery vocabulary. "We build what your work stands on." | Good odds | Cool to the point of cold; one syllable of consonant cluster |
| **Apse** / **Nave** | The forms of the church body. *Nave* is from *navis*, ship — church and ship in one syllable. | Open | **Nave is a homophone for "knave."** Kill it. Apse is too clipped and obscure |
| **Vault** | The engineered curve that lets a ceiling span further than stone should. Structural daring as beauty. | Poor — banking, fintech, storage | Too claimed |

### C. Material & surface

| Name | Rationale | Domain prior | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Gesso** | The white ground brushed onto a panel *before* the painting — the invisible preparation that determines whether the work survives five centuries. A studio that does the unglamorous foundational layer so the beautiful thing lasts. Painters and designers know it instantly. | Good odds across TLDs | Spelling from audio: JESS-oh invites "Jesso." Real cost on a podcast |
| **Patina** | What time gives to well-made things. Warm, material, design-literate. | `.com` held; `patina.studio` | Implies age, which sits oddly on a firm that ships fast |
| **Slate** | Surface, roof, and clean start. Excellent radio test. | Poor — heavily used | Slate the magazine, Slate the many startups. Too crowded |

### D. Deadpan single nouns (the studio convention)

| Name | Rationale | Domain prior | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Character** | A typographic character *and* moral character — the exact double a Christian design firm wants, with neither meaning shouting. | Poor | Character is a well-known SF design studio. Direct collision in your category |
| **Measure** | The typographic measure (a line's length), the standard, and "the measure of a man." | Poor as `.com` | Generic and hard to search |
| **Proof** | The printer's proof; the proving of a thing; the evidence. Punchy, short, design-native. | `.com` held; `proof.studio` | Reads QA/testing to an engineering audience — wrong first impression |
| **Register** | When printing plates align perfectly, the work is "in register." Precision as the whole point. | Poor | Reads clerical/records |

### E. The eponymous option

Worth naming explicitly because it's the **single most common convention in high-end design firms** and v1 skipped it: Wolff Olins, Pentagram, Porto Rocha, Collins, Hoefler. A two-surname lockup — `[Surname] & [Surname]` — signals *these two people are the product*, which is true of you and is the hardest thing for a competitor to copy. It also solves the trust problem the podcast ad is trying to solve, since the host is vouching for two actual humans.

**Cost:** it's permanently tied to you both, it's harder to sell or scale past, and it gives the brand no metaphor to hang the "good, beautiful, and true" story on. Recommend considering it only as `[Surname] & [Surname]` for the firm with a named practice or product underneath.

## Phase 4 — Finalist development

### 1. Tracery — *the recommendation*

**Origin story:** In a cathedral window, the tracery is the carved stone that holds the glass — and it's both the thing keeping the wall standing and the most beautiful thing in the room. Nobody who built one thought of structure and beauty as separate jobs. Neither do we.

**Taglines:**
- *Structure and beauty, one thing.*
- *What holds the light.*
- *Drawn, then built.*

**In the ad:** "That's where Tracery comes in. They're seasoned builders — full-stack engineering paired with design and product expertise…" The name has done half the sentence's work before the host finishes it.

**Why it beats the v1 pick:** Keel said *we build sturdy things*. Tracery says *we build sturdy things that are worth looking at, and we don't think those are two departments* — which is the actual differentiator of a design-heavy dev firm, and the actual content of "good, beautiful, and true."

**Domain strategy:** `tracery.com` is worth a real check — decent odds it's parked and acquirable. Otherwise `tracery.studio` (the TLD is itself a positioning signal), `tracery.co`, `withtracery.com`.

**Watch for:** delicacy. Pair it with a solid, unfussy visual identity — heavy type, real structure — so it reads *stonework*, not *lace*.

---

### 2. Vellum — *the material pick*

**Origin story:** When someone in the twelfth century intended a book to outlive them, they wrote it on vellum. The surface was chosen first, because that's what decided whether the work survived. We think about software the same way.

**Taglines:** *Made to outlast us.* · *The surface things last on.* · *Built for the long read.*

**Strengths:** best radio test in the finals, two clean syllables, tactile, immediately design-world, and the manuscript association does the Christian work silently.

**Watch for:** vellum.pub is established book software with a design-adjacent audience — this needs a genuine Class 42 clearance check before you commit.

---

### 3. Gesso — *the theology-of-craft pick*

**Origin story:** Gesso is the ground a painter lays before any paint touches the panel. Nobody ever sees it, and it's the reason the painting is still there five hundred years later. Most of what we do for founders is gesso.

**Taglines:** *The layer underneath.* · *Prepared to last.* · *Foundation first, beauty after.*

**Strengths:** the most distinctive and most available name in the set; a genuinely humble story that fits stewardship without preaching it.

**Watch for:** JESS-oh will be typed "Jesso" by some fraction of every podcast audience. If you love it, the ad has to say "G-E-S-S-O" — which is not fatal (hosts spell URLs constantly) but is a permanent small tax.

---

### 4. Colophon — *the best story, encumbered*

**Origin story:** At the back of a well-made book there's a colophon: who made this, on what press, in what type, in what year. It's the maker standing behind the finished thing — and it only exists on books that got finished.

That is the single tightest story in either brainstorm. The problem is that Colophon Foundry is a respected London type house, and your design-literate prospects are precisely the people who know it. Not a legal conflict; a "wait, the type foundry?" conflict, every time.

---

### 5. Plinth — *the confident minimal option*

**Origin story:** A plinth is what a made object stands on so it can be seen properly. We build the thing your work stands on.

Short, dry, gallery-confident, wide open on domains. It's the coldest name in the set — right if you want the identity to carry the warmth, wrong if you need the name to.

---

## Recommendation

**Tracery**, with **Vellum** as the close alternate and **Gesso** as the distinctive outside pick.

Tracery is the only candidate in either pass that argues the firm's actual thesis in a single word: structure and beauty made by the same hands, for something meant to last, in a tradition where that was a form of worship. It's design-world native, it clears the radio test, it isn't encumbered, and it has room to grow past two people.

**Retire:** Rubric (Rubrik homophone), Character (existing SF studio), Nave (knave), Slate / Signature / Measure / Imprint (too generic to own).

## Before committing

- [ ] USPTO Class 42 + 35 on Tracery, Vellum, Gesso — plus phonetic variants ("Tracerie," "Velum," "Jesso")
- [ ] Real availability and price on `.com` / `.studio` / `.co` for the top three
- [ ] Handles: X, LinkedIn, Instagram, GitHub, Dribbble (**Dribbble and Behance matter now** — a design-heavy firm with no handle on either looks off)
- [ ] Run the finalists past two designers and two founders separately; they will disagree, and the founders' read is the one that pays
- [ ] **The ad test:** record the 30-second spot with each finalist. The one you don't wince at is the name.
