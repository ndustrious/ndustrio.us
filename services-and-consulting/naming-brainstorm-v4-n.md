# Naming Brainstorm v4 — The Letter N

Fourth pass, constrained to names beginning with **N**. Taste calibration carries forward unchanged from v3: *Latinate or Greek terms of art, borrowed from a discipline with a real technical vocabulary, naming a structure or a relation rather than an object or an action. Cool, precise, three syllables or fewer. Nothing announced.*

Prior passes: `naming-brainstorm-v1-craft-trades.md` (retired), `naming-brainstorm-v2-design.md`, `naming-brainstorm-v3-taste.md`.

---

## First, the two on the table

### Novitas — correctly ruled out, and the whole "new" thread is thin

Novitas Solutions is a Medicare Administrative Contractor processing claims for several states — a large, federally contracted entity in continuous public use. That's not a squatter you buy out.

Worth knowing before you spend more time on the *meaning*: the "newness" vein is unusually crowded for a short word.

- **Nova** — thoroughly claimed, and it drags the Chevrolet joke
- **Novum** — retired anyway under the v3 rule against `-um` endings rhyming with Stratum
- **Novation** — a genuinely beautiful term of art: in contract law, replacing an old obligation with a wholly new one, **by consent of every party**. That is the new covenant in legal vocabulary. But Novation is a well-known music hardware brand, and your buyers include people with a synthesizer in the room
- **Renovaré** — taken, and taken by Richard Foster's spiritual formation ministry, which is squarely inside your audience

My read: newness is the weakest available theme for this brand anyway. Your copy doesn't sell *new* — it sells **finished, sound, and lasting**. A name meaning "new" would argue against the thesis that a prototype isn't a product.

### Nomos — your instinct is right, and the reason is better than the sound

The "no-" phonetics are a real cost. But there's a stronger objection underneath.

*Nomos* is the specific Greek word Paul sets **against** grace — law as the thing that cannot save, throughout Romans and Galatians. For a Christian firm, naming yourself Nomos plants your flag on the wrong side of the central contrast in Pauline theology, and the fraction of your audience most likely to notice is the theologically literate fraction. Every well-catechized listener does a small double-take.

Not a close call. Retire it, and retire it for that reason rather than the phonetics — the phonetics alone might have been survivable.

## Phase 2 — N candidates in the vein

### Sacred architecture

| Name | Term of art in | What it means | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Narthex** | Church architecture | The entrance hall of a church — the threshold space between the world outside and the sanctuary proper. Historically where catechumens stood: admitted, but not yet all the way in | **The one that maps the business.** Your entire pitch is the distance between "it runs" and "people rely on it." Narthex names that gap as architecture. Wide open |
| **Newel** | Stair construction | The central post a spiral stair winds around and structurally depends on | Good meaning — the core everything ascends around. Spelling from audio is shaky ("Nuel," "Neuel") |
| **Nervure** | Gothic vaulting / botany | The stone rib of a vault; the vein of a leaf | The `-ure` ending matches Ligature exactly, and it's Tracery's structural cousin. But "nerve" drags, and the pronunciation is contested in English |

| **Nave** | Church architecture | The body of the church; from *navis*, ship | Retired in v2 — homophone with *knave* |

## Phase 4 — Finalist development

### 1. Narthex — *the recommendation*

**Origin story:** In an old church the narthex is the room you stand in before the sanctuary — inside the doors, not yet all the way in. It's where the not-yet-baptized waited: genuinely part of the thing, genuinely not finished. Most founders who come to us are standing in exactly that room with a prototype in their hands. Our whole job is the walk from there to the other side.

**Taglines:**
- *The room before the room.*
- *From the threshold to the sanctuary.*
- *We know the distance.*

**In the ad:** "That's where Narthex comes in. They're seasoned builders — full-stack engineering paired with design and product expertise…" Two clean syllables, no spelling ambiguity worth worrying about, and it doesn't sound like any software company currently operating.

**Why it wins under this constraint:** it's the only N word that is simultaneously (a) a real term of art in a design discipline — architecture, (b) a Christian word that costs nothing with a secular listener who just hears an unusual firm name, and (c) **a literal description of the gap your business exists to close.** Ligature argued that design and engineering are one form; Narthex argues something more valuable to a founder, which is that you understand precisely where they're standing.

**Alongside Stratum:** "Stratum, by Narthex" — a Greek architectural singular over a Latin geological one. Different disciplines, same mind. Exactly the relationship v3 was after.

**Watch for:**
- The `-thex` cluster is the hardest sound in any finalist across four passes. Say it aloud fifty times before you decide; it either settles into something distinctive or it doesn't.
- Obscurity cuts both ways — most listeners won't know the word, so the name carries no meaning until you tell the story. In a host-read that's a feature. On a business card it's a blank.
- **Check church-software specifically.** If a collision exists it will be in parish administration or giving platforms, which is where a word like this would already have been used. I haven't verified this; it belongs at the top of the clearance list.

**Domain strategy:** wide open by any reasonable prior. Go for `narthex.com` first — genuine odds it's parked and cheap — then `narthex.studio`, `narthex.design`.

### 2. Newel — *the quiet structural alternate*

A newel is the post at the center of a spiral stair. Every tread is fixed to it; the whole ascent is possible because one vertical thing is carrying it. It's Armature's argument in an architectural vocabulary: the invisible core that makes the beautiful thing stand.

**Origin story:** A spiral stair works because of the newel — the single post at its center that every step is anchored to. Nobody photographs the newel. Nothing gets up to the next floor without it.

**Taglines:** *The post at the center.* · *What the whole stair turns on.*

**Watch for:** spelling from audio is the weak point. Listeners will type "Nuel" or "Neuel." If Narthex fails clearance, this is where I'd go next — with the domain doing corrective work.

---

### 3. Nimbus — *the warm option, if ownability weren't a problem*

The nimbus is the disc of gold behind a saint's head in an icon — the painter's convention for the presence of God on an ordinary human figure. Art-historical, immediately spellable, warmer than anything else under N.

It fails on ownership, not on beauty. "Nimbus" has been used repeatedly in cloud infrastructure, so a technical audience hears weather-and-servers before it hears iconography, and the trademark landscape in Class 42 is likely crowded. Included because if the clearance surprises you in a good direction, it's the most *likeable* name in this pass.

---

### 4. Nonpareil — *the best idea you probably can't use*

A nonpareil is a 6-point type size. It also means, in plain English, *having no equal*. A typographic term of art that is simultaneously a claim of excellence is a genuinely rare object, and no one else has it.

Four syllables, a French spelling, and a pronunciation most Americans hedge on. On a podcast-led brand this is disqualifying, and I'd rather say so than let it survive on charm. Noted here because if the channel strategy ever shifts away from audio, it comes back.

---

## Recommendation

**Narthex** — contingent on a church-software clearance check.

It's the only candidate under this constraint that holds all four things at once: the v3 taste register, a clean radio test, an open field, and a meaning that describes the exact business you're in. The threshold framing is also more sellable than any metaphor in the previous three passes, because it centers the founder's position rather than your process.

If Narthex is taken, **Newel** carries nearly the same argument and needs only a domain that spells it for people.

Retire under N: Nomos (Pauline problem), Novitas (contracted entity), Numen (design-collective collision), Neume and Nonpareil (radio), Nous and Nave (homophones).

## A note across four passes

The strongest candidates have each argued a different thing, and it's worth choosing which argument you want to make:

| Name | The argument it makes |
|---|---|
| **Ligature** | *Design and engineering are one form here, not a handoff* — a claim about how the studio is built |
| **Armature** | *The beautiful thing is structurally sound underneath* — a claim about the product |
| **Narthex** | *We know exactly where you're standing, and we know the walk* — a claim about the founder |

The third is the one a founder hears themselves in.
