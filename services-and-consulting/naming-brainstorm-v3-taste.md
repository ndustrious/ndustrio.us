# Naming Brainstorm v3 — Calibrated to Taste

Third pass, converging rather than diverging. Inputs: Ligature named as the favorite from v2, and **Stratum** as a product name that has held up over months of living with it.

Prior passes: `naming-brainstorm-v1-craft-trades.md` (retired), `naming-brainstorm-v2-design.md`.

---

## What Ligature and Stratum have in common

Two data points, but they agree so precisely that the pattern is usable:

| | Ligature | Stratum |
|---|---|---|
| Origin | Latin *ligatura* < *ligare*, to bind | Latin *stratum*, a layer spread out |
| Vocabulary | Typography / paleography | Geology / systems |
| Kind of word | **A term of art** — a word with an exact meaning inside a discipline | Same |
| What it names | A **relation** (two forms fused into one) | A **structure** (an ordered layer) |
| Register | Cool, precise, classical | Cool, precise, classical |
| Faith content | None on the surface | None on the surface |

**The taste, stated:** *Latinate terms of art, borrowed from a discipline that has a real technical vocabulary, naming a structure or a relation rather than an object or an action. Three syllables or fewer of exactness. Cool, not warm. Nothing announced.*

That's a coherent and unusually good instinct, and it explains why v1 and most of v2 missed:

- **v1 failed on register.** Keel, Shipwright, Whetstone are Anglo-Saxon trade words — warm, physical, folksy. Your ear wants Latinate and exact.
- **Most of v2 failed on kind.** Vellum, Gesso, Slate, Patina are *materials* — substances, not terms of art. Tracery and Plinth are *objects*. Ligature is the only name in v2 that describes a **relation between two things**, which is the actual product of a two-person firm where design and engineering are fused.

So the target for this pass is narrow and clear.

## Brand architecture, before names

Stratum changes the problem. You are no longer naming one thing; you're starting a **system**, and the company name has to sit above Stratum without competing with it.

Three rules follow:

1. **Different vocabulary, same register.** Ligature (typography) and Stratum (geology) are drawn from different disciplines but sound like they came from the same mind. That's the ideal relationship — family resemblance without confusion. Two geology words, or two type words, would blur.
2. **Avoid the `-um` ending entirely.** Fulcrum, Datum, Modulus, Continuum are all on-taste but would rhyme with Stratum and make the company sound like a second product. This retires several otherwise strong candidates.
3. **Avoid the layer metaphor.** Substrate, Bedrock, Lamina, Sediment are Stratum's synonyms. The parent can't be a restatement of the child.

Test every finalist by saying it: *"Stratum, by ___."* If the two names sound like siblings rather than parent and child, the name is wrong.

## Phase 4 — Finalist development

### 1. Ligature — *your pick, and it holds up*

**The thing v2 didn't surface.** *Ligature* comes from Latin *ligare*, to bind. So, on the oldest and most widely held etymology of the word — argued by Lactantius and endorsed by Augustine — does **religion**: *re-ligare*, to bind back, to bind fast. (Cicero derived *religio* from *relegere* instead, and scholars still argue it; but the binding etymology is the one the Christian tradition carried for sixteen centuries.)

Which means Ligature is quietly the same root as religion — and nothing on the surface says so. That is exactly the register the copy already works in: faith as the thing shaping how the work is done, not a label applied to it.

**Origin story:** A ligature is what happens when two letterforms are cut into one because they read better fused than they do side by side. That's the studio: design and engineering aren't a handoff here, they're one form. And *ligature* shares its root with *religion* — both from *ligare*, to bind. We didn't plan that. We kept it.

**Taglines:**
- *Bound together.*
- *One form, not a handoff.*
- *What binds the work.*

**In the ad:** "That's where Ligature comes in. They're seasoned builders — full-stack engineering paired with design and product expertise…" The name has already made the pairing argument.

**Two honest problems:**

1. **Search hygiene — the real risk, and not the one v2 named.** I flagged "surgical connotation" last pass; that was the wrong concern. Designers hear typography first and the medical sense is merely clinical. The genuine hazard is that *anti-ligature* is an entire product category in behavioral-health construction, and *ligature marks* is forensic vocabulary. A cold search for the bare word surfaces a grim second page. Brand context disambiguates hard — nobody landing on a design studio's site is confused — but you should look at the actual SERP before committing, and the fix if it's ugly is to own a domain that always carries context (`ligature.studio`, `ligature.design`).
2. **Length.** Three syllables, and the longest name in any of the three passes. On a host-read it needs the URL spelled, or a shorter domain doing the work.

**Domain strategy:** `ligature.com` is likely held. `ligature.studio` and `ligature.design` are both strong here — the TLD is itself a positioning signal for a design-heavy firm, and it fixes the search-context problem in one move.

### 2. Armature — *the alternate I'd make you consider before you commit*

Same ending, same register, same discipline-borrowed exactness — and it fixes both of Ligature's problems while arguably making a sharper argument.

An armature is the rigid internal skeleton a sculptor builds before a single piece of clay goes on. Nobody sees it. It's the entire reason the figure stands up. A design-heavy firm whose actual differentiator is that the beautiful thing is also *structurally sound underneath* has no better word available.

**Origin story:** Before a sculptor touches clay, they build an armature — the steel skeleton the whole figure hangs on. It's invisible in the finished work and it's the only reason the work is still standing. We build the armature.

**Taglines:** *The structure underneath.* · *Beautiful, and it stands up.* · *Built on a frame.*

**Why it might beat Ligature:** clean search results, no grim second page, and it argues *engineering under design* rather than *two people working together* — the first is a claim about the product, the second is a claim about the org chart. Founders buy the first.

**Why it might not:** Ligature has the *religare* root, and Armature has no faith layer at all. If the hidden theology matters to you, that's decisive.

---

### 3. Tessera — *the best hidden story in three passes*

A tessera is a single tile in a mosaic. In Rome it was also a token: a small tile broken in two between host and guest, each keeping a half, so that years later either party — or their children — could produce it and the two halves proving they fit was the whole proof of the bond. It's where the phrase *tessera hospitalis* comes from.

Mosaic is the native art of the early church. A tessera is one small piece, placed with care, meaningless alone and part of something enormous in aggregate. And the token sense is a covenant made of two halves that fit.

**Origin story:** In Rome, two friends would break a small tile in two and each keep half, so that years later the halves fitting was proof of the bond. It was called a tessera. It's also the word for a single tile in a mosaic — one small piece, set with care, part of something none of us sees the whole of.

**Taglines:** *One piece at a time.* · *Set with care.* · *The halves fit.*

**Watch for:** slightly softer and more Italian-sounding than Ligature or Armature; "Tessera" is used by a handful of small firms — worth a clearance pass. Also excellent alongside Stratum: two Latin singulars from different disciplines.

---

### 4. Escapement — *the outside pick*

The escapement is the mechanism that releases a clock's stored energy in measured beats. Without it the mainspring dumps everything at once and the hands spin uselessly; with it, you get time kept true for a hundred years. It's the single best metaphor available for *shipping in disciplined increments* — which is the literal mechanic of the subscription model in the productized copy.

Longest name here and the hardest radio test, which is why it isn't higher. But it's clean on search, wide open on domains, and no one else has it.

---

## Recommendation

**Ligature is a good name and you should be allowed to keep it** — the taste read behind it is sound, the *ligare/religare* root gives it a layer none of the alternates have, and it makes the design-and-engineering-as-one-form argument in a single word.

Before you commit, do exactly one thing: **look at a real search results page for the bare word.** That's the only finding that would change my answer. If the results are tolerable, take Ligature on `ligature.studio` or `ligature.design`. If they're grim, **Armature** is waiting — same taste, same shape, cleaner ground, and a slightly stronger claim.

Keep **Tessera** in the drawer regardless. If Ligature and Armature both fall down, it's the one with the story you'd most enjoy telling for ten years.

## Footnote — a story asset regardless of the name

Vitruvius held that architecture must have *firmitas, utilitas, venustas* — firmness, usefulness, beauty. It is the oldest surviving statement of "good, beautiful, and true" as a **standard for made things**, it's two thousand years old, and it belongs in your About page under whatever name you choose.
