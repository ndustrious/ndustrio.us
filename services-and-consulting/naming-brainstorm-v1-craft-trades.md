# Naming Brainstorm v1 — Craft & Trades Vein

> **Status: superseded, kept for the good parts.** This pass drew from the shipyard and the job site — a builder's register that read too industrial for a design-heavy firm. See `naming-brainstorm-v2-design.md` for the second pass. The diagnosis of `ndustrio.us` below still stands and was not re-litigated in v2.

Generated with the Lunour naming framework, grounded in `sales-marketing-copy-v2.md`, `sales-marketing-copy-alt-productized.md`, and the podcast ad draft.

## Phase 1 — Brand discovery (inferred from the copy; correct anything wrong)

| | |
|---|---|
| **What we do** | A two-person studio that turns founders' early ideas and AI-built prototypes into real, shipped products — engineering, design, and product judgment in one team. |
| **Primary customer** | Christian entrepreneurs and solo founders with an idea they can't shake, often holding a working-but-fragile AI-built prototype. Reached primarily through podcast host-reads. |
| **Core feeling on first hearing** | *Trust, borne by craft.* Not delight, not speed, not power — the feeling that these are grown-ups who finish things and tell the truth. |
| **Brand personality** | Craftsmanlike · Candid · Enduring |
| **Names we're moving away from** | `ndustrio.us` — a domain hack. See diagnosis below. |
| **Hard constraints (derived)** | 1. **Must survive the radio test** — the ad is the primary channel; a listener has to spell it correctly from hearing it once, while driving. 2. **Must work in third person plural** — "That's where ___ comes in. *They're* seasoned builders." 3. **Must read as a firm of humans**, not a SaaS product or an AI tool. 4. **Legible to Christians, dignified to everyone else** — no in-group jargon that costs credibility with a secular co-founder, investor, or hire. 5. **Must scale** past two people and past services, in case the subscription model or a product spins out. |

### Diagnosis of the current name

There's real equity in *industrious* — the meaning is genuinely on-brand (diligent, hardworking, a stewardship virtue). The failure isn't the word. It's the wrapper:

- **The domain hack breaks the primary channel.** A host says "n-dustrious dot us" and nobody types it right. Every ad dollar leaks at the URL.
- **It's a 2012-era convention** (`del.icio.us`, `bit.ly`) that now reads as dated and slightly cheap — the opposite of "good, beautiful, and true."
- **"Industrious" is a worker's virtue, not a maker's promise.** It describes effort, not outcome. This brand's promise is *finishing* — carrying something across the distance between "it runs" and "people rely on it."
- **It says nothing about craft, trust, or lastingness** — the three things every other line of copy is about.

**What the new name must unlock:** spellable-from-hearing; a craft/finishing image rather than an effort image; quiet Christian resonance that doesn't require a decoder ring; room to grow.

And the standard reassurance: less than 1% of the addressable market has ever heard "ndustrio.us." Optimize for the 99% who haven't.

## Phase 4 — Finalist development

### 1. Keel — *the recommendation*

**Origin story:** A ship starts as a single timber laid down the center — the keel. Everything else is built onto it, and nothing floats without it. We exist for the part of the work where an idea stops being a sketch and becomes something that can carry weight.

**Taglines:**
- *Built to carry weight.*
- *From prototype to shipped.*
- *Lay it true.*

**In the ad:** "That's where Keel comes in. They're seasoned builders — full-stack engineering paired with design and product expertise..." — one syllable, lands clean, and "keeldotcom-slash-whatever" is unmissable at 60 mph.

**Domain strategy:** `keel.com` is out of reach. Go `keel.studio`, `keelandco.com`, `buildwithkeel.com`, or `keel.co` — and be aware there is a UK developer-tooling company called Keel; check Class 42 before committing.

**Watch for:** it's abstract, so the meaning has to be told once. That's a feature in a host-read — the story *is* the ad.

---

### 2. Shipwright — *the tightest meaning fit*

**Origin story:** A shipwright doesn't design boats on paper. They build the thing that goes to sea, and their name is on it when it does. We're named for a trade because that's what this is.

**Taglines:**
- *Ideas, made seaworthy.*
- *We finish what you started.*
- *Craftsmen for the last mile.*

**Domain strategy:** the exact `.com` is likely in marine or logistics use. `shipwright.studio`, `shipwrightco.com`, `theshipwright.co`.

**Watch for:** heavier nautical load than Keel, and "wright" reads a touch old-world. Also the most likely of the finalists to have a naming collision — search first.

---

### 3. Cubit — *the quiet scripture pick*

**Origin story:** The ark and the temple were both built to a measure — the cubit. Sacred work has always been work done to a standard, and the standard is the point.

**Taglines:**
- *Built to the measure.*
- *Good, beautiful, and true — to spec.*
- *A standard you can build on.*

**Domain strategy:** best `.com` odds of the finalists; `cubit.studio` and `cubit.co` as fallbacks.

**Watch for:** mild archaism, and Christians will hear "ark" before they hear "temple." Test whether that association helps or amuses.

---

### 4. Cairn — *the legacy pick*

**Origin story:** When Israel crossed the Jordan they stacked twelve stones on the far bank, so that when children asked what they meant, someone could tell them. We build things meant to be standing when someone asks.

**Taglines:** *Built to be found later.* · *Stones that stay stacked.* · *Mark the way.*

**Watch for:** spelling. In a host-read the URL has to carry it, and "Karn" is a real risk.

---

### 5. Handiwork — *the theology pick*

**Origin story:** "We are God's handiwork, created in Christ Jesus to do good works." We think that verse is a job description.

**Taglines:** *Made by hand, built to last.* · *Good work, plainly done.*

**Watch for:** the craft-fair connotation. If the concern is that Handiwork undersells two senior engineers, that concern is correct.

---

## Recommendation

**Keel**, with **Shipwright** and **Cubit** as the live alternates.

Keel wins on the constraint that actually costs money — the radio test — while keeping a genuine craft image, an honest Christian resonance that never becomes in-group jargon, and enough abstraction to survive a pivot into a product or a bigger team. It's also the only finalist that is one syllable, which matters more than it sounds like it should.

## Before committing

- [ ] USPTO Class 42 and Class 35 search on the top three, plus phonetic variants
- [ ] Real domain availability and price check (`.com`, `.studio`, `.co`, `buildwith[name].com`)
- [ ] Handle check on X, LinkedIn, Instagram, GitHub
- [ ] **The three-day test:** say the finalist once to five people in the target audience. Ask them three days later to spell it and say what the company does.
- [ ] **The ad test:** record yourself reading the 30-second spot with each finalist inserted. The one that doesn't make you wince is the name.
