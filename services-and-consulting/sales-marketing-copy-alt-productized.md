# Sales & Marketing Copy — Alternative Approach: The Productized Subscription

An alternative to the "ladder of trust" in `sales-marketing-copy-v2.md`. Same beliefs, same two-person team, same clients — a different **business shape**.

Where v2 sells discrete, fixed-price *projects* (Founder Call → Roadmap → Launch Package → Custom → Partner), this version sells one thing: **a flat-rate product-development subscription you can pause anytime.** It's modeled on Brett Williams' DesignJoy and the "Productize Yourself" method.

## Internal framing — read first

### The model in one breath

One flat monthly price. You send requests — designs, features, fixes, whole builds. We work them **one at a time**, ship fast, and revise until they're right. Add as many requests to the queue as you like. **Pause the month you don't need us; resume when you do.** No contracts, no scope negotiations, no hourly billing, no meetings you don't want.

### What DesignJoy actually is (so we adapt it, not cargo-cult it)

Brett Williams runs DesignJoy solo. A client pays a flat monthly fee, submits unlimited design requests, and Brett works them one at a time off a board — most delivered in about 48 hours, unlimited revisions, pause or cancel anytime, essentially no calls. Its genius is **simplicity as the product**: one price, one queue, zero friction. "Productize Yourself" is his method for turning any service business into that shape.

### The one honest problem, and how we solve it

DesignJoy sells *design* — a landing page, a logo, a set of screens. Those tasks are naturally bite-sized, which is why "one at a time, 48 hours" works cleanly.

**We sell shipped software.** Turning a prototype into a real product is a longer arc than a single 48-hour task. If we copied the promise literally, we'd be lying.

So we translate the mechanic instead of the marketing:

- **The queue stays.** One active request at a time. You always know exactly what we're working on.
- **48 hours becomes the rhythm, not the whole feature.** Small requests — a screen, a fix, a component, an integration, copy, a design pass — land in ~48 hours. Larger features get **broken into shippable increments** and delivered the same way: one at a time, fast, visible. You watch the product grow in a steady cadence instead of waiting weeks for a big reveal.
- **"Unlimited" is honest because throughput is honest.** Unlimited *requests in the queue*, worked at the pace two senior people can genuinely ship — not unlimited simultaneous work. We say that plainly.

### The second honest problem: judgment at scale, and the roster cap

Our tooling multiplies how much we can *build* — that's why a two-person studio can carry ten-plus subscriptions where a solo designer carries a handful. But it doesn't multiply the parts the copy actually sells: senior review of every shipped increment, honest counsel on whether a request is the right thing to build, and holding each client's product in our heads. **Review and judgment are the real bottleneck, and we defend them structurally: a fixed roster cap.**

- We take a set number of active subscriptions at a time — a number we choose so every client genuinely gets the 48-hour rhythm, senior eyes on everything, and counsel that knows their product. We raise the cap only when we've hired or our throughput has genuinely grown, never to catch a revenue wave.
- **When the roster is full, we run a waitlist.** Publicly, plainly, with a "spots available" indicator on the site. This is DesignJoy's playbook and it works *because it's true* — scarcity as an honest operating constraint, not a marketing trick. The moment the cap is fake, this whole section violates our own copy rules.
- The cap also finishes the pause-risk argument: at a ten-plus roster, pauses become statistical noise you price for, and the waitlist backfills — a paused or churned slot is offered to the next founder in line, so revenue holds without pressure-retention tactics.

### Why this shape fits our beliefs (not just our margins)

- **Pause-anytime is anti-waste.** We won't bill you to sit idle. You pay for the weeks we're actually building. That's stewardship of *your* money, stated as a feature.
- **Flat and transparent.** No open-ended hourly meter — the thing v2 already refuses. This just makes it structural.
- **One outcome, we own it working.** Same promise, standing instead of per-project.

### Where it diverges from the ladder (the tradeoffs, honestly)

- **Less counsel-forward, more shipping-forward.** The Roadmap sold *judgment as the deliverable*. Here judgment is folded into the build. We keep the free Founder Call as the on-ramp so the advisory relationship still starts every engagement — but the paid product is "we build, continuously," not "we advise, then quote."
- **Revenue smooths but softens.** Predictable MRR instead of $8.5–15K lumps; but clients can pause, so a slow month is a real slow month. The model rewards a full roster of active subscriptions — which is exactly what the roster cap + waitlist is for: at a ten-plus roster, pauses average out and the waitlist backfills open slots.
- **Fit is narrower.** Perfect for founders who have *ongoing* build needs. A one-and-done "ship this prototype and I'm gone" founder is served worse here than by the Launch Package — worth keeping that as a side door (see §5).

### How it works

**Four steps, then repeat.**

1. **Subscribe.** One flat monthly rate. No contract, no scoping call required to start.
2. **Request.** Add as much as you like to your queue — designs, features, fixes, whole builds. Written down, async, no meeting required (though we're glad to talk).
3. **Receive.** We build your requests **one at a time** and ship fast. Small ones in about 48 hours; larger features broken into shippable pieces and delivered the same way — one after another, so you always see the product moving.
4. **Revise.** Not right yet? Send it back. We revise until it is.

**Pause anytime.** Building in bursts? Pay for the weeks you're active and pause the rest. Your queue and your product wait for you.

You can *queue* everything. We *work* one request at a time. That's not a limitation we're hiding — it's the discipline that makes the work good. It means the thing on our bench right now has our full attention, ships fast, and gets built plumb, square, and true, instead of five half-finished features drifting at once. When it's done, we pull the next card. You always know exactly what we're building.

### What you can put on the queue

Anything in your product's life, from first build to steady growth:

- **Infrastructure** — deployment, monitoring, the plumbing that makes it production-grade
- **The small stuff** — copy, a settings page, an integration, the polish that separates a demo from a product

If it moves your product forward, it belongs on the queue.

One hour. Free. Genuinely useful.

## Copy rules observed (carried from v2 + new for this model)

**Carried from v2:**

- Christian framing is load-bearing everywhere: stewardship, vocation, craft-as-service, legacy, "good, beautiful, and true." Avoid prosperity/get-rich language — legacy is enduring worth, not wealth.
- We state plainly that we are Christians ourselves — not just that we serve Christians.
- Builders *and* counselors — never "not consultants." Advisory value stays named (the Founder Call; judgment folded into every request).
- No mechanism talk: agent orchestration never appears; outcomes and speed only.
- No self-undercutting of the prospect's work: their prototype is "a real start," never slop.
- Prices public and specific; the free call is the only unpriced rung and must deliver real value, not a pitch.
- Ownership shelf: framed only as *the client owns everything* — never as an upsell or a mechanism.

**New for the productized model:**

- **Simplicity is the product.** Resist adding tiers, add-ons, or scope tables. One subscription, one optional Double, one free call, one side door. Every element you add taxes the model's whole appeal.
- **Never overstate throughput.** "Unlimited requests," always — "unlimited work," never. The 48-hour promise covers single requests; larger features are honestly framed as increments. If we can't ship it that way, we don't imply we can.
- **Pause-anytime is a belief, not a growth hack.** Frame it as refusing to bill for idle weeks (stewardship), not as a slick retention trick.
- **Async is an offer, not a wall.** "Meetings only if you want them" — we never sound like we're dodging the client. Counsel stays available; it's just not mandatory overhead.
- **The queue metaphor is the product's face.** "One at a time," "pull the next card," "on the bench" — keep it concrete and visible. Transparency of *what we're building right now* is the trust mechanism that replaces the ladder's rung-by-rung earning.
- **The cap is real or it doesn't exist.** The roster cap defends the review-and-judgment bottleneck — the one thing our tooling doesn't multiply. The spot counter reflects actual capacity, the waitlist is a real queue worked in order, and the scarcity line in the ad airs only while the cap binds. Fake scarcity would poison every honesty claim on the page. Raise the cap when throughput genuinely grows; never for a revenue wave.
- **Pause and cap coexist by disclosure.** Pause-anytime stays absolute on billing; the spot-hold period (`[HOLD PERIOD]`) is the one boundary, stated plainly in the FAQ, never sprung on anyone, and softened by front-of-line re-entry. If we ever have to choose between a returning pauser and a waitlist convert, the copy has already told both of them the rule.
