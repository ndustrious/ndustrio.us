# Ligature — Domain & Trademark Risk Assessment

**Date:** 2026-08-14
**Status:** Findings for joint decision, before further brand investment
**Bottom line:** The name has a prior user in our exact business with fourteen years of priority. This should be settled before the logo is finished.

---

## Why this document exists

The plan was straightforward: we'd chosen Ligature (see `naming-brainstorm-v3-taste.md`), Matt was starting the logo, and the remaining task was finding a domain that's easy to say out loud. That research turned up something that outranks the domain question, so this document covers both — domains first, since that's what prompted it, then the name-availability problem it exposed.

*Preliminary research, not legal advice. Section 4 recommends what a lawyer should actually be asked.*

---

## 1. Domain availability

Checked ~95 domains via RDAP, then probed the taken ones over HTTP to see whether anything real was hosted there.

**v3's two recommendations are both gone.** `ligature.studio` redirects to an existing design agency; `ligature.design` is held.

### Available and recommended

| Domain | Why |
|---|---|
| **`ligature.build`** | Cleanest spoken option. No near-neighbor domain to leak typos to, no spelling ambiguity. The name argues design, the TLD argues engineering. |
| **`weareligature.com`** | Standard agency convention (wearesocial, weareimpero). A `.com` never provokes "dot what?" — the strongest performer on the say-it-once test. |
| **`madebyligature.com`** | Same bulletproofing, and it describes what we do. |

### Also available

`ligature.io`, `ligature.agency`, `ligature.house`, `ligature.group`, `ligature.team`, `ligature.company`, `ligature.works`, plus compound `.com` forms (`builtbyligature`, `withligature`, `hiligature`, `ligaturehq`, `ligaturegroup`, `ligaturehouse`, `tryligature`, `ligatureco`).

### Two traps worth naming

- **`ligature.co`** is available and is the best-looking string on the entire list. It's also the one to avoid: say "dot co" out loud and a real share of listeners type "dot com," which is registered to someone else and sits blank. Leads vanish silently.
- **`ligature.works`** has the nicest meaning (a *works* = a workshop, which echoes the ndustrio.us heritage), but `ligature.work` singular is a live unrelated AI product. Same leakage problem.

**If we keep the name:** `ligature.build` as primary, `weareligature.com` for email and typo capture redirecting to it, and defensively grab `ligature.co` and `ligature.io` (~$60/yr total) so neither becomes someone else's.

## 3. What the law actually turns on

### The federal register is mostly clear

| Mark | Class | Status |
|---|---|---|
| LIGATURE.COM | 042 — website design, hosting, programming | **Dead** — cancelled 2007, un-revivable |
| THE LIGATURE | 016 — paper goods, stationery | Live — registered & renewed |
| SILVERSTEIN LIGATURE | 015 — woodwind parts | Live — unrelated |
| "Ligature" claimed by Arcadea IP LLC (`ligature.vc`) | unverified | **Needs checking** |

No live federal registration obviously blocks a design/dev studio in Class 35/42. The cancelled `LIGATURE.COM` registration is even mildly encouraging — the USPTO granted this exact word for website design once before. One loose end: `ligature.vc` states "Ligature is a registered trademark of Arcadea IP LLC," and we couldn't surface the serial number through public search. That needs a TSDR lookup.

### But registration isn't where the risk lives — priority is

Ligature Creative shows no ™ or ® anywhere, which sounds like good news and isn't. **Trademark rights come from use, not registration.** They have fourteen years of continuous use in identical services with a national web presence. We would be the junior user.

The likelihood-of-confusion factors line up badly:

- **Dominant element identical** — "Creative" is descriptive and distinguishes nothing. Both marks are functionally LIGATURE.
- **Services identical** — branding and web development, both firms.
- **Channels identical** — web presence, referral, the same kind of buyer.

### On state of formation

Worth addressing directly, because the intuition is common and reasonable: *we'd be registering in Idaho or the Carolinas, so a Colorado firm shouldn't matter.*

Secretary of State registration and trademark rights are **two separate systems**. When Idaho says "Ligature LLC is available," it means no Idaho entity has claimed that string in the Idaho corporate registry. The clerk doesn't search the USPTO, doesn't search common-law use, and doesn't look at Colorado. It's a database dedupe. Consequently:

- Forming Ligature LLC in Idaho doesn't stop anyone from making us stop using the name — including in Idaho.
- Forming Ligature LLC in Idaho doesn't grant the right to *use* "Ligature" as a brand at all. Entity name and trademark are different grants from different authorities.

There **is** a real geographic doctrine behind the intuition — Tea Rose–Rectanus — which lets a good-faith junior user in a remote market keep operating where the senior user's reputation hasn't reached. A bakery in Boise and a bakery in Raleigh coexist forever. It requires two conditions, and a web-native services firm fails both:

1. **Geographic remoteness.** Ligature Creative sits in Denver, but their market doesn't — they sell over the web nationally. We'd be structured identically; ndustrio.us is a website selling to founders anywhere. Neither firm has a territory. The doctrine was built for markets that physically couldn't reach each other, and two websites always reach each other.
2. **Good faith.** The defense requires not knowing about the senior user. As of this document, we know. That's not a reason to regret looking — a formal clearance search produces the same knowledge, and proceeding in ignorance just means finding out later — but the remote-good-faith defense isn't available to us going forward.

And if either party federally registers, geography stops mattering entirely: a federal registration confers nationwide constructive use from the filing date.

### Honest odds

This is not a prediction of a lawsuit. Two small studios routinely coexist for years, because enforcement costs money and nobody notices. The realistic bad outcomes are quieter:

- A cease-and-desist that arrives *after* we've built equity in the name.
- A federal application blocked or opposed whenever we finally file.
- Ordinary business friction from being the fourth Ligature in design.

### The cost that lands even if nobody ever complains

Set the law aside. We'd be the **fourth Ligature in design.** Someone hears the name once at a conference and searches it — they find a Denver agency, a studio, and a VC fund before they find us. Brand recall is diluted from day one: *"Ligature? The Denver one?"*

And the positioning quietly contradicts itself. The whole argument for this name is precision — a word chosen so exactly that design and engineering fuse into a single form. Sharing it with three other design firms undercuts that argument in the most visible possible place. v3 scored Ligature well on ownability; that score was made without this information.

## 4. Options

Ranked as recommended:

1. **Pivot to Armature.** v3 already kept it in reserve — same register, same `-ure` ending, and v3 judged its claim (*engineering beneath design*) the sharper of the two. Run the same domain + register + live-brand sweep on it first. If it's clean, this is the cheapest possible moment to switch: nothing has shipped, and the logo isn't finished.
2. **Get a clearance opinion on Ligature.** Roughly $500–$2,000 flat fee for a knockout search plus written opinion. The right move if we're committed to the name — but do it *before* any further brand spend, not after.
3. **Modify the mark** so the distinctive element is ours rather than the shared word. Weakens the elegance that made the name worth having in the first place.
4. **Proceed as-is, no federal filing, accept junior-user status.** Cheap today, expensive precisely when it hurts most.

### Cheap checks before paying anyone

- [ ] USPTO's own search UI at [tmsearch.uspto.gov](https://tmsearch.uspto.gov) — search `ligature`, live marks only, classes 035 and 042. Settles the Arcadea question in about five minutes.
- [ ] Read `ligcreative.com`'s client list against our target market. Direct overlap raises the risk materially; no overlap doesn't remove it, but it changes the practical calculus.
- [ ] Decide whether to commission the Armature sweep before Matt commits the logo.

## Method

Domain status came from RDAP queries against the authoritative registries, with HTTP probes to separate parked domains from live businesses. Trademark data came from public mirrors of the USPTO register — USPTO's own API is key-gated, and the aggregators that mirror it (Justia, uspto.report) block automated access, so a handful of records are sourced from search-result snippets rather than fetched directly. Any record marked "needs checking" above was not independently verified.
